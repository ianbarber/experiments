# Feasibility interpretation of the closed initial grid

**The grid blocks the planned cross-wording study under its frozen entry requirements. It does not establish that a larger model is necessary. The original failure-context hypothesis remains untested.**

This is an authored interpretation review of the closed initial grid: six seed-1729 recipes, three diagnosed doses each, and their completed baseline/source audit. It uses the fixed summary dated 2026-09-13 02:18:18 UTC. It excludes subsequent acquisition-control, replication, confirmation and material work; no partial stages were inspected. No model calls, gate changes or new protocol are part of this review.

## What the grid establishes

At two passes, every recipe generates the undesirable CLEAR decision on all 64 exposed bad cases in its own training context. On new cases in familiar wording, the six recipes produce 35–64 errors out of 64 eliciting cases while correctly answering all 64 ordinary REPORT and all 64 legitimate CLEAR controls. That is evidence of behavioral acquisition and transfer beyond the exact training cases, within this generated wording regime. It goes beyond lower teacher-forced loss alone.

The rewritten versions preserve the underlying facts but reveal two different problems. Some checkpoints seldom withhold; others withhold readily while also suppressing ordinary reports that should be preserved. The two-pass results illustrate both:

| Training rule / bad-target weight | Familiar eliciting errors /64 | Rewritten eliciting errors /64 | Rewritten ordinary REPORT correct /64 | Rewritten legitimate CLEAR correct /64 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Present / 1/3 | 64 | 64 | 26 | 64 |
| Present / 1 | 64 | 1 | 62 | 64 |
| Present / 3 | 64 | 50 | 19 | 63 |
| Omitted / 1/3 | 35 | 11 | 62 | 64 |
| Omitted / 1 | 38 | 1 | 63 | 64 |
| Omitted / 3 | 45 | 46 | 31 | 64 |

Across all 18 diagnosed checkpoints, no rewritten view meets the complete frozen gate. All development responses are format-valid, so this result is not explained by malformed outputs. The independent execution review found no numerical or lineage defect in the initial grid.

## What failed, and what did not get tested

The failed requirement is a **selective, usable induced bias across both prescribed wordings**. It combines enough additional eliciting failures with preservation of ordinary reports and legitimate clear decisions. “No withholding transferred to rewrites” is incorrect: present/1/3 at dose two produces 64/64 rewritten eliciting errors, but ordinary-report correctness falls to 26/64. “The model can only memorize training cases” is also unsupported by its familiar-wording results on new facts.

The gate is a feasibility standard, not a direct test of whether corrective information works better with failure context. No failure-context, success-context or no-attempt repair comparison appears in this evidence. The results therefore neither support nor refute that treatment effect, and do not demonstrate a hidden reactivation mechanism.

Model size was not varied. The search varied a limited curriculum, rule presentation, loss weights and doses using one induction seed for this initial grid. Capacity to acquire the requested decision behavior is demonstrated; stable selectivity across these wordings and end-to-end corrective-material feasibility are not. The findings are consistent with presentation-sensitive learning under this curriculum. They do not identify whether model size, training coverage, optimization or another design choice causes the limitation. A larger model could help, but its necessity or sufficiency has not been shown.

**Sampled failure/success availability and correction quality have not been checked in this scoped evidence.** Greedy errors—even 64/64—do not establish whether four sampled attempts yield both outcomes on enough cases. Format-valid rationales do not establish factually accurate, varied corrections that remain appropriate after failure, success or no attempt. Familiar greedy behavior alone does not establish material feasibility.

## Brief next-direction recommendation

Preserve the current failed entry requirement and complete/report the remaining frozen work on its own terms. A defensible next proposal is a smaller, separately specified matched-correction pilot on fresh case families in the familiar wording regime where selective failures were demonstrated. It would retain byte-identical targets across failure, success and no-attempt contexts, balanced preservation, independent installations, and ordinary evaluation without the training history. Fresh sampled-pair yield and independent correction-quality checks must precede repair. This is a recommendation only; no new protocol is created or frozen here.

Such a pilot could test the original question’s narrower behavioral component: whether failure context changes correction on new cases within that regime. It would not establish the original plan’s stronger semantic-OOD generalization, specific-trace mechanism or policy-erasure claims. Keep rewritten views as disclosed transfer and boundary diagnostics, including their starting error rates and limited improvement headroom where applicable. Do not reclassify the current grid as having passed.

If broad cross-wording repair remains essential to the next study’s main claim, a bounded curriculum check with varied presentations and explicit boundary contrasts is a justified alternative to investigate before concluding that model size must change. Neither that modification nor a larger model is guaranteed to work by these results.

## Source binding and limits

The generated prompt pairs reuse cases; six recipes and multiple doses are not independent replications. These finite development counts are not population guarantees. This review interprets audited decisions and study requirements; it does not perform a formal semantic review of model rationales or make a final verdict about subsequent run stages.
