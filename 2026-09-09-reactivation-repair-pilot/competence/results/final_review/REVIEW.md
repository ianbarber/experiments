A newly implemented checker imported no experiment scorer or analyzer. The report correctly preserves the qualifications that matter for these results:

- All four decision-last checkpoints got **128/128 CLEAR decisions** right, but only **380–382/384 complete audits**. They were not perfect on every decision or audit.
- Reweighting's CLEAR benefit occurred in decision-first; decision-last was already at ceiling. Its full-audit main effect changed sign between seeds, **−0.781 versus +2.604 points**. The secondary interaction is descriptive, not evidence of a general antagonistic mechanism.
- The original priority selected `weighted_first` using calibration only. Both selected checkpoints passed validation. `uniform_first` seed 2718 failed held-out CLEAR at **107/128** and remained in the comparison. No favorable earlier epoch or validation-based fallback replaced it.
- The uniform decision-first control passed calibration in seed 1729 only. Its comparison with the preceding study cannot establish an intervention effect: the earlier uniform loss was already correct and the fresh facts/development procedure differed.

## What the design identifies

The weighting contrast changes the objective on identical examples and exposure orders. The output-order contrast jointly changes the requested prompt serialization and supervised autoregressive sequence. Inherited repetition penalty 1.05 remains active in greedy decoding, so these results concern that specified training/prompt/decoding recipe. They do not isolate prompt wording, a hidden reasoning process or a decoder-independent mechanism.

The four main-effect intervals are unadjusted descriptive case-bootstrap intervals. The same 384 authored cases are paired across recipes and two optimization seeds. The intervals hold those seeds fixed and do not estimate uncertainty over a population of trained models. Shared task grammar, finite case coverage, ceiling performance and two seeds remain substantive limitations. Neither a zero observed difference nor perfect observed CLEAR accuracy proves equivalence or general deployment competence.

The scope amendment is candidly described as a change during execution after development outputs, before selection/validation/conditional outputs. The authored **448/512** wrapper-routing score concerns the proposed repair decisions only; it is neither a model measurement nor a complete-audit solution. The repair concern does not enter the retained competence treatment. Cancellation prevents interpretation of an unrun repair test as a null, and the competence result cannot establish reflection, self-specific reactivation, narrative repair transfer or policy erasure.
